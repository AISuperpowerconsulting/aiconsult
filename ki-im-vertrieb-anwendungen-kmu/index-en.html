AI in sales: 7 use cases every SME can start using right away

By Adrian Althaus · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

In sales, a lot of time goes not into customer conversations but into everything around them: writing quotes, following up, entering notes into the CRM, preparing meetings. AI can now take over much of this work. Here are seven use cases that almost any SME can put into practice without an IT project.

Why sales benefits in particular

Sales is largely about language: emails, quotes, meeting notes, arguments. That is exactly what AI assistants are good at. According to the AXA SME labour market study 2026, correspondence (42%) and translation (47%) are already among the most common AI uses in Swiss SMEs. The next step is to build this deliberately into your sales processes.

7 use cases of AI in sales

1. Create quotes faster

From meeting notes, a price list and a quote template, a draft with a cover letter tailored to the customer's needs is ready in minutes. Your team checks prices and terms and approves it.

2. Follow-up emails and customer correspondence

Following up often gets lost in day-to-day work. AI drafts suitable follow-ups after a meeting, a quote or a trade fair, in your company's tone and with reference to the conversation.

3. Prepare customer meetings

Before a meeting, AI summarises what is known about the customer: previous purchases, open points, industry, likely questions. Your sales team goes into the meeting prepared instead of searching for documents.

4. Visit reports and CRM entries

Bullet points or a voice note after the meeting become a clean visit report with next steps, ready for the CRM. This saves time and ensures knowledge does not stay in individual employees' heads.

5. Objections and sales arguments

AI helps you collect typical objections and prepare answers, for example on price, delivery time or competing products. New team members get up to speed with the arguments faster.

6. Analyse and comment on sales figures

Revenue per customer, region or product can be analysed faster. AI highlights what stands out and drafts comments for the monthly report. The calculations themselves belong in Excel or an analysis tool.

7. Communicate in several languages

Many Swiss SMEs sell in German, French, Italian and English. AI translates quotes, product information and emails to a good standard. Important documents should still be proofread by someone who speaks the language.

Get started in four steps

  1. Collect the time drains. Ask your sales team which tasks come up every week and take the most time.
  2. Pick two use cases. Start with tasks that are frequent and low-risk, such as follow-up emails and visit reports.
  3. Build templates. Good prompts and example texts in your company's tone matter more than the tool. Store them centrally so everyone uses them.
  4. Set rules. Record in a short AI usage policy which customer data may go into which tool and who reviews the results.

Example prompt: follow-up email after a customer meeting

You are a sales consultant at [company]. Write a short follow-up email to [customer's role] after our meeting on [date]. Notes from the meeting: [bullet points].

Requirements: polite and personal, no more than 120 words. Summarise the two most important points of the meeting, state the agreed next step and suggest a concrete date. Do not invent any facts that are not in the notes.

Limits and risks

  • Data protection: customer data is personal data. Use a business version in which inputs are not used for training, and only enter the information that is needed.
  • Check the facts: AI can state convincing but wrong information, for example on prices or technical details. Anything that goes to customers is checked first.
  • Tone and relationships: standard texts quickly feel generic. The personal touch and the relationship with the customer remain your team's job.
  • Clear responsibility: define who approves AI output. This gives confidence to your team and your customers.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI tools are suitable for sales?

To get started, AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot or Claude in a business version are enough. Good templates and clear rules in the team matter more than the tool.

May I enter customer data into an AI tool?

Only with care. Use a business version in which inputs are not used for training, enter only the data that is needed, and record the rules in an AI usage policy based on the Swiss Data Protection Act.

How quickly does AI in sales pay off?

With quotes, emails and meeting notes, teams often notice the benefit in the first week because these tasks come up every day. What matters is that the team works with its own templates.

Conclusion

AI does not replace the conversation with the customer, but it takes a lot of the surrounding work off your team. If you start with two well-chosen use cases and set clear rules, you will see the benefit quickly and without a big project.
